# LVH II: The Great Drought, 1876-1878

## Victoria's Ghosts

[Robert Lytton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Bulwer-Lytton,_1st_Earl_of_Lytton) and [Richard Temple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sir_Richard_Temple,_1st_Baronet) used capitalism and the idea of the free market as excuses for colonial genocide in India during the mass famines of 1876-1878.

To put the low rations in perspective, the "Temple ration" in Madras in 1877 was ~1600 cal, compared to the approved Indian male diet in 1981 being ~3900 cal. This ration was less than that of the [Buchenwald concentration camp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buchenwald_concentration_camp). Temple accused his medical officers of prioritizing public health over public finances too much.

> "Everything," he lectured, "must be subordinated ... to the financial consideration of disbursing the smallest sum of money consistent with the preservation of human life."

Temple sent people to work camps for working on colonial railways, digging canals, or breaking stones. On top of the hunger and famine, these camps were breeding grounds for disease and had insanely high mortality rates.

> As Temple’s most dogged critic, Dr. Cornish, pointed out, monthly mortality was now equivalent to an annual death rate of 94 percent. Post-mortem examinations, moreover, showed that the chief cause of death--"extreme wasting of tissue and destruction of the lining membrane of the lower bowel"--was textbook starvation, with full-grown men reduced to under sixty pounds in weight.

In jails, prisoners were fed better than temple wages, and it was not uncommon for people to try to end up in jail because of this.

The [Poona Sarvajanik Sabha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poona_Sarvajanik_Sabha) estimated that as much as 36-48% of the population had decilined in Madhee, Mohol, and Indi. Buckingham after ordering a rough census to comply with public opinion noted that in the Madras districts at least 1.5 million had died by 1877.
